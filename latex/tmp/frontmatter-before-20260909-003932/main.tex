\begin{abstract}
Small witnesses do not necessarily yield inexpensive SNARK proofs:
random challenges turn small-field witness entries into large-field
values, increasing both arithmetic and memory costs.
We present \methodname, a framework for proving relations over small
finite fields while confining large-field computation to shorter
vectors.
Our main technique, \emph{polynomial packing}, reduces a
length-\(N=m\kappa\) vector relation over \(\Field\) to a
length-\(m\) relation over an extension \(\ExtensionField\) of degree
\(\kappa\).
The prover encodes the relation as a low-degree polynomial identity,
computes its row products over \(\Field\), and proves the shorter
relation obtained by evaluation at a random point in \(\ExtensionField\).

We give packed reductions for inner and Hadamard products, together
with a permutation argument and a polynomial commitment interface
that authenticates challenge-dependent packings of the witness.
These components yield arguments for a single circuit execution and
for multiple executions of the same circuit.
For \(|\Field|\geq\kappa\) and \(m\geq\kappa\), the inner- and
Hadamard-product reductions use \(O(\kappa N)\) base-field operations
and \(O(N)\) base-field coordinates of memory under schoolbook
arithmetic and precomputed packing bases, excluding commitments and
openings.
The constructions support fields of either characteristic through
suitable intermediate extensions.
At \(N=2^{30}\) over an 8-bit base field, our component benchmarks
show prover speedups of \(5.60\times\) for inner products and
\(4.31\times\) for Hadamard products over the small-field-first
baseline, with measured resident memory reduced by a factor of
\(4.48\) in both cases.
\end{abstract}
