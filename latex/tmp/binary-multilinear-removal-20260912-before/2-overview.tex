\section{Technical Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

Our goal is to verify a relation over a small field without running
the extension-field proof on every witness coordinate.
For \(N=m\kappa\) and \([\ExtensionField:\Field]=\kappa\),
vector polynomial packing reduces length-\(N\) inner- and Hadamard-product
claims over \(\Field\) to length-\(m\) inner products over
\(\ExtensionField\).
There are two requirements: the polynomial encoding must preserve
the claimed relation, and the shortened operands must remain bound
to a commitment made before the packing challenge.
We first explain the encoding and its cost, then the opening
protocol that provides this binding.

\subsection{Vector Polynomial Packing}
\label{sec:dualPhase}

Assume \(m,\kappa\) are powers of two and \(\kappa\geq2\).
Arrange the committed vectors \(\bm a,\bm b\in\Field^N\) as
\(\kappa\) columns of length \(m\):
\[
    \bm A=\concat{\bm a^{(1)},\ldots,\bm a^{(\kappa)}},
    \qquad
    \bm B=\concat{\bm b^{(1)},\ldots,\bm b^{(\kappa)}}
    \in\Field^{m\times\kappa}.
\]
Let \(\bm X=(X_1,\ldots,X_s)\) for \(s\geq1\), and choose any
\(\Field\)-linearly independent polynomials
\(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_\kappa\in\Field[\bm X]\).
Writing \(\bm\phi=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_\kappa)^\top\), encode
the rows as vector-valued polynomials:
\begin{equation}
    \bm g_A(\bm X)=\bm A\bm\phi(\bm X),
    \qquad
    \bm g_B(\bm X)=\bm B\bm\phi(\bm X)
    \in\Field[\bm X]^m.
\label{eq:overview-packing}
\end{equation}
Linear independence makes this encoding injective as a map to
formal polynomials; the basis need not be univariate.
Evaluation at \(\ext{\bm x}\in\ExtensionField^s\) then replaces
each row of \(\kappa\) base-field elements by one
extension-field element:
\[
    \bm A\in\Field^{m\times\kappa}
    \quad\longmapsto\quad
    \bm g_A(\bm X)
    \quad\xrightarrow{\bm X=\ext{\bm x}}\quad
    \ext{\bm a}=\bm g_A(\ext{\bm x})\in\ExtensionField^m.
\]
Packing the operands alone does not preserve the relation:
multiplying their encodings introduces products between different
columns. We choose the basis so that the desired products can be
recovered either as a designated coefficient or at designated
interpolation nodes.
The prover completes the resulting identity with degree-bounded
auxiliary polynomials whose prescribed form cannot change that
coefficient or those node values.
Only after these polynomials are fixed do we evaluate the identity
at a random extension-field point, obtaining the shorter claim.
The degrees and product structure of the chosen basis determine
the soundness error and prover cost.

\subsection{Packing with Extension-Field Bases}

Let \(q=|\Field|\) and
\([\ExtensionField:\Field]=\kappa\).
Choose \(\ext{\theta}\in\ExtensionField\) of degree
\(\kappa\) over \(\Field\), and let
\[
    \ext{\bm{\beta}}^\dagger
    =(1,\ext{\theta},\ldots,\ext{\theta}^{\kappa-1})^\top.
\]
Let \(\ext{\bm{\beta}}\) be its trace-dual basis, so that
\[
    \trace(\ext{\beta}_u\ext{\beta}_v^\dagger)
    =\delta_{uv}.
\]
For matrices \(\bm A,\bm B\in\Field^{m\times\kappa}\),
whose vectorizations are
\(\bm a,\bm b\in\Field^N\), where \(N=m\kappa\), define
\[
    \ext{\bm a}=\bm A\ext{\bm\beta},
    \qquad
    \ext{\bm b}=\bm B\ext{\bm\beta}^\dagger.
\]

\paragraph{Inner products.}
Trace duality gives
\[
    \trace\bigl(
        \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\rangle
    \bigr)
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\kappa}
        A_{i,\ell}B_{i,\ell}
    =
    \langle\bm a,\bm b\rangle.
\]
To prove \(c=\langle\bm a,\bm b\rangle\), the prover sends
\[
    \ext c
    \coloneqq
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\rangle.
\]
The verifier checks \(\trace(\ext c)\EqOrNot c\), and the
parties prove the length-\(m\) extension-field claim
\[
    \ext c
    =
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\rangle.
\]
Assuming the packed operands are authenticated, this
reduction introduces no additional algebraic soundness error.

\paragraph{Hadamard products.}
We consider the projected claim
\[
    \bm\Xi^\top(\bm A\circ\bm B)=\bm C,
    \qquad
    \bm\Xi\in\Field^{m\times\kappa},
    \quad
    \bm C\in\Field^{\kappa\times\kappa}.
\]
Here, \(\bm\Xi\) may represent the base-field coordinates
of an extension-field tensor
\(\ext{\bm\xi}=\bm\Xi\ext{\bm\beta}\).
The matrices \(\bm A,\bm B,\bm C,\bm\Xi\) are fixed before
the challenges below are sampled.

To obtain sufficiently many effective checks while
sharing their computation, fix a linear code with generator
matrix
\[
    \bm G\in\Field^{\kappa\times t},
    \qquad
    t=O(\kappa),
\]
and minimum distance \(\Delta\geq\kappa\).
We require both maps
\(\bm u^\top\mapsto\bm u^\top\bm G\) and
\(\bm v\mapsto\bm G\bm v\) to admit
\(\Field\)-linear arithmetic circuits of size \(O(\kappa)\).
These circuits and the packing bases are precomputed.

For each \(j\in[t]\), set
\[
    \bm\omega_j=\bm G_{:,j},
    \qquad
    \bm\Omega_j=\diag(\bm\omega_j).
\]
The verifier independently samples
\[
    \bm\eta_j\sample\Field^\kappa,
    \qquad
    \bm\lambda_j=\bm\Xi\bm\eta_j\in\Field^m.
\]
Define
\[
    \ext{\bm b}_j
    =\bm B\bm\Omega_j\ext{\bm\beta}^\dagger.
\]
The prover sends the scalar claims
\[
    \ext c_j
    \coloneqq
    \left\langle
        \bm\lambda_j\circ\ext{\bm a},
        \ext{\bm b}_j
    \right\rangle
    \in\ExtensionField.
\]
Since \(\bm\lambda_j\) has base-field entries,
\[
    \trace(\ext c_j)
    =
    \bm\eta_j^\top
    \bm\Xi^\top(\bm A\circ\bm B)\bm G_{:,j}.
\]
The verifier therefore checks
\[
    \trace(\ext c_j)
    \EqOrNot
    \bm\eta_j^\top\bm C\bm G_{:,j}
    \qquad\text{for every }j\in[t].
\]

For a fixed nonzero error matrix
\[
    \bm M
    =\bm\Xi^\top(\bm A\circ\bm B)-\bm C,
\]
the matrix \(\bm M\bm G\) has at least \(\Delta\) nonzero
columns: any nonzero row of \(\bm M\) encodes to a codeword
of weight at least \(\Delta\).
Each such column passes its independent random
\(\bm\eta_j\)-check with probability \(1/q\).
Consequently, the true inner-product values satisfy all
trace checks with probability at most
\[
    q^{-\Delta}\leq q^{-\kappa}.
\]

\paragraph{Batching.}
After receiving all \(\ext c_j\), the verifier samples
\(\ext x\sample\ExtensionField\).
For each row \(i\in[m]\), define
\[
    (\ext{\bm v}_i)_j
    =\ext x^{j-1}\lambda_{i,j},
    \qquad j\in[t],
\]
and
\[
    \ext b_i^*
    =
    \sum_{\ell=1}^{\kappa}
        B_{i,\ell}\ext{\theta}^{\ell-1}
        (\bm G\ext{\bm v}_i)_\ell.
\]
The claims reduce to one length-\(m\) inner product:
\[
    \sum_{j=1}^{t}\ext x^{j-1}\ext c_j
    =
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}^*\rangle.
\]
If any transmitted \(\ext c_j\) is incorrect, the
discrepancy is a nonzero polynomial in \(\ext x\) of
degree at most \(t-1\).
Thus, conditioned on authentication of the terminal
operands, the reduction contributes at most
\[
    q^{-\Delta}
    +\frac{t-1}{|\ExtensionField|}
\]
to the soundness error.
Errors from the initial Hadamard projection and the
terminal proof are accounted for separately.

\paragraph{Prover complexity.}
The prover generates all \(\ext c_j\) by processing
one row at a time.
For row \(i\), form
\[
    u_{i,\ell}
    =
    B_{i,\ell}\ext a_i\ext{\theta}^{\ell-1},
    \qquad \ell\in[\kappa],
\]
and encode
\[
    \bm e_i^\top=\bm u_i^\top\bm G.
\]
Then
\[
    \ext c_j
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}e_{i,j}.
\]
In the polynomial representation of \(\ExtensionField\),
multiplication by \(\ext{\theta}\) costs
\(O(\kappa)\) base-field operations.
Hence, generating all \(u_{i,\ell}\) costs
\(O(\kappa^2)\) base-field operations per row.
The linear encoding circuit uses only additions and
multiplications by base-field constants, so encoding
the extension-field vector \(\bm u_i\) also costs
\(O(\kappa^2)\) base-field operations.
Computing all \(\lambda_{i,j}\) and accumulating the
messages has the same cost.

The prover forms \(\ext{\bm b}^*\) using the transposed
encoding circuit followed by Horner evaluation at
\(\ext{\theta}\), again in \(O(\kappa^2)\)
base-field operations per row.
Including basis conversion and a terminal inner-product
prover using \(O(m)\) extension-field operations,
the total arithmetic cost under schoolbook multiplication is
\[
    O(m\kappa^2)=O(\kappa N).
\]
Processing rows sequentially requires
\(O(N+\kappa^2)\) base-field coordinates of working memory,
which is \(O(N)\) when \(m\geq\kappa\).
These bounds exclude commitments and openings.

\paragraph{Commitment interface.}
The reduction requires authentication of its packed
operands and of the claimed projection \(\bm C\).
In particular, \(\ext{\bm b}^*\) uses packing weights
that depend on both the row of \(\bm\Xi\) and the column
of \(\bm B\).
Authenticating its evaluations against a fixed-basis
commitment to \(\bm B\) therefore requires an additional
consistency reduction.
The arithmetic bounds above do not establish the cost
of this opening interface.


\subsection{Instantiating the Packed Reduction}

We first describe the univariate instantiations used in our
protocols, then extend the Hadamard encoding to multivariate bases.

\paragraph{Inner products.}
To encode \(c=\langle\bm a,\bm b\rangle\), assign opposite monomial
powers to matching columns. Choose
\(\bm\phi(X)=(1,X,\ldots,X^{\kappa-1})^\top\) and pair
\(\bm g_A(X)\) with \(\bm g_B(X^{-1})\).
For two blocks:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\langle\bm a^{(1)}+X\bm a^{(2)},
          \bm b^{(1)}+X^{-1}\bm b^{(2)}\rangle\\
 &\quad=
   \langle\bm a^{(1)},\bm b^{(1)}\rangle
   +\langle\bm a^{(2)},\bm b^{(2)}\rangle
   +X\langle\bm a^{(2)},\bm b^{(1)}\rangle
   +X^{-1}\langle\bm a^{(1)},\bm b^{(2)}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
The constant coefficient is the inner product, and every
cross-column term has a nonzero exponent.
For arbitrary \(\kappa\), the claim therefore holds if and only if
there exist
\(h_+,h_-\in\Field[X]\), each of degree at most \(\kappa-2\), satisfying:
\begin{equation*}
    \langle\bm g_A(X),\bm g_B(X^{-1})\rangle
    =c+Xh_+(X)+X^{-1}h_-(X^{-1}).
\end{equation*}
The auxiliary terms contain only nonzero powers, so no choice of
\(h_+\) or \(h_-\) can change the claimed constant coefficient.
Their degree bounds ensure that a false claim gives a low-degree
nonzero residual after clearing negative powers.
After receiving \(h_+\) and \(h_-\), the verifier samples
\(\ext x\in\ExtensionField^\times\).
Writing \(\ext{\bm a}=\bm g_A(\ext x)\) and
\(\ext{\bm b}=\bm g_B(\ext x^{-1})\), it remains to prove:
\[
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\rangle
    =c+\ext xh_+(\ext x)+\ext x^{-1}h_-(\ext x^{-1}).
\]
The verifier computes the right-hand side from the received
polynomials; the length-\(N\) inner product has become a
length-\(m\) claim.

\paragraph{Hadamard products.}
Unlike an inner product, a Hadamard claim must check all
coordinate products. We first reduce it to a weighted scalar claim
using a random multilinear evaluation.
Given a committed \(\bm C\in\Field^{m\times\kappa}\), project
\(\bm C=\bm A\circ\bm B\) with random tensor weights
\(\ext{\bm l}\in\ExtensionField^m\) and
\(\ext{\bm r}\in\ExtensionField^\kappa\).
Each weight is a Kronecker product of two-entry MLE vectors.
This projection has error at most \(\log_2N/|\ExtensionField|\).
It suffices to authenticate
\(\ext c=\ext{\bm l}^{\top}\bm C\ext{\bm r}\) and prove:
\[
    \ext c=\ext{\bm l}^{\top}(\bm A\circ\bm B)\ext{\bm r}.
\]
The prover supplies claimed column products
\(\ext c_j=\langle\ext{\bm l},
\bm a^{(j)}\circ\bm b^{(j)}\rangle\), and the verifier checks
\(\ext c=\ext{\bm c}^{\top}\ext{\bm r}\).

For \(|\Field|\geq\kappa\), choose \(\kappa\) distinct nodes
\(\mathcal H=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\kappa\}\subseteq\Field\)
and the Lagrange basis satisfying
\(\psi_j(\alpha_i)=1\) for \(i=j\) and \(0\) otherwise.
Use \(\bm\phi=(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_\kappa)^\top\) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:overview-packing}, and set
\(g_C(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\ext c_j\psi_j(X)\) and
\(\Psi(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}(X-\alpha_j)\).
At \(\alpha_j\), the packed vectors recover column \(j\).
All column claims therefore hold if and only if the residual is
divisible by \(\Psi\):
\begin{equation*}
    \ext{\bm l}^{\top}
      \bigl(\bm g_A(X)\circ\bm g_B(X)\bigr)-g_C(X)
    =\Psi(X)h(X),
    \qquad \deg h\leq\kappa-2,
\end{equation*}
where \(h\in\ExtensionField[X]\).
Since \(\Psi\) vanishes on \(\mathcal H\), no choice of \(h\)
can change the claimed values at the interpolation nodes.
This is the counterpart of excluding the constant coefficient
from the auxiliary terms in the inner-product encoding.
After \(\ext{\bm c}\) and \(h\) are fixed, the verifier samples
\(\ext x\in\ExtensionField\setminus\mathcal H\).
With \(\ext{\bm a}=\bm g_A(\ext x)\) and
\(\ext{\bm b}=\bm g_B(\ext x)\), the reduced claim is:
\[
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\circ\ext{\bm l}\rangle
    =g_C(\ext x)+\Psi(\ext x)h(\ext x).
\]

In both constructions, an incorrect identity has a nonzero
residual of degree \(O(\kappa)\), after clearing negative powers
in the inner-product case. Random evaluation therefore incurs
error \(O(\kappa/|\ExtensionField|)\).
The resulting length-\(m\) inner-product proof uses \(O(m)\)
extension-field operations and leaves two multilinear-evaluation
(MLE) claims on its operands~\cite{asiacrypt/CCYY25}.
Both constructions thus use the same proof and opening interface.

\paragraph{Multivariate Hadamard packing.}
The interpolation argument extends to an ordered point set
\(S=\{\bm\alpha_1,\ldots,\bm\alpha_\kappa\}\subseteq\Field^s\).
For a given basis \(\bm\phi\), interpolation on \(S\) requires
the evaluation matrix \(\bm V=(\phi_j(\bm\alpha_i))_{i,j\in[\kappa]}\)
to be nonsingular. The normalized basis is then:
\[
    \bm\psi(\bm X)=\bm V^{-\top}\bm\phi(\bm X),
    \qquad
    \bm\psi(\bm\alpha_j)=\bm e_j.
\]
Thus Hadamard interpolation requires linear independence of the
restrictions to \(S\), whereas polynomial packing itself requires
only formal linear independence.
Use \(\bm\psi\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:overview-packing} and set
\(g_C(\bm X)=\ext{\bm c}^{\top}\bm\psi(\bm X)\).
The vanishing ideal of \(S\) is:
\[
    I(S)=\{h\in\Field[\bm X]:
          h(\bm\alpha)=0\text{ for every }\bm\alpha\in S\}.
\]
It captures precisely the polynomial corrections that preserve
all node values. The column claims are therefore equivalent to:
\[
    f(\bm X)\coloneqq
    \ext{\bm l}^{\top}
      \bigl(\bm g_A(\bm X)\circ\bm g_B(\bm X)\bigr)
      -g_C(\bm X)
    \in I(S)\ExtensionField[\bm X],
\]
where the extended ideal allows extension-field coefficients in
the correction polynomials.

For a grid \(S=D^s\) with \(D\subseteq\Field\) and
\(|D|^s=\kappa\), choose the tensor-product Lagrange basis,
of degree below \(|D|\) in each variable.
Writing \(\Psi(T)=\prod_{\alpha\in D}(T-\alpha)\), the grid ideal
and the resulting certificate take the form:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    I(D^s)&=\langle\Psi(X_1),\ldots,\Psi(X_s)\rangle,\\
    f(\bm X)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^s\Psi(X_\ell)h_\ell(\bm X).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:overview-multivariate-hadamard}
\end{equation}
Successive division by these monic generators gives
\(h_\ell\in\ExtensionField[\bm X]\) with total degree at most
\(2s(|D|-1)-|D|\).
After \(\ext{\bm c}\) and these degree-bounded polynomials are
fixed, evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:overview-multivariate-hadamard}
at a uniformly random \(\ext{\bm x}\in\ExtensionField^s\).
This again gives a length-\(m\) inner product, with right-hand
side \(g_C(\ext{\bm x})+
\sum_{\ell=1}^s\Psi(\ext x_\ell)h_\ell(\ext{\bm x})\).
A false column claim makes the residual nonzero, so this
identity test has error at most
\(2s(|D|-1)/|\ExtensionField|\).

The univariate certificate is the case \(s=1\).
Over \(\Field=\mathbb F_2\), one may instead take
\(D=\{0,1\}\) and \(s=\log_2\kappa\), with generators
\(X_\ell^2-X_\ell\).
This removes the requirement \(|\Field|\geq\kappa\) for
Hadamard interpolation, but changes the product space: binary multilinear
row polynomials have products with up to
\(3^s=\kappa^{\log_2 3}\) coefficients.
Appendix~\ref{appx:binary-hadamard} gives a mixed-tensor-basis
construction of all \(s\) quotients. Given the row weights, it
uses \(O(m\kappa^{\log_2 3})\) bit operations and the same order
of extension-field XORs, with no general extension-field
multiplication during quotient generation.

Alternatively, for \(q=|\Field|<\kappa\), the univariate
construction can use a small intermediate field
\(\Field\subseteq\mathbb K\subseteq\ExtensionField\).
With \(\kappa\) a power of two, choose
\([\mathbb K:\Field]=O(\log_q\kappa)\) so that
\(\mathbb K\) contains enough evaluation points for the row
products. The prover forms and aggregates their value tables
over \(\mathbb K\), then interpolates only the aggregate.
Tabulating intermediate-field multiplication and the fixed
linear map from input blocks to samples removes the logarithmic
arithmetic overhead. For \(m\geq\kappa\), this gives
\(O(N\kappa)\) online base-field operations and \(O(N)\)
working coordinates, while retaining the original
\(\Field\)-valued witness and its commitment.
Section~\ref{sec:hadamard} accounts separately for the reusable
tables and their construction.

\subsection{Prover Complexity}
\label{sec:highLevelEfficiency}

The following bounds concern the base-field univariate
instantiations above, with \(q\geq\kappa\) for Hadamard products.
The tabulated intermediate-field variant has the same online
time bound with additional read-only tables; the multivariate
variant depends on its basis and product space.
The reduction saves work only if constructing the auxiliary
polynomials is cheaper than proving the uncompressed relation.
The key is to multiply the row polynomials \emph{before} evaluating
them in \(\ExtensionField\).
For inner products, the prover computes \(h_+\) and
\(h_-\) entirely over \(\Field\).
For Hadamard products, it first multiplies the row polynomials over
\(\Field\), then accumulates their coefficients with the
extension-field weights \(\ext{\bm l}\) and divides the residual
by \(\Psi\).
This order keeps the row multiplications over the base field even
though the weighted identity has extension-field coefficients.

With precomputed bases, these steps cost
\(O(m\kappa^2)=O(\kappa N)\) base-field operations and \(O(N)\)
mixed-field operations. Evaluating the packed vectors also costs
\(O(N)\) mixed-field operations, followed by \(O(m)\) extension-field
operations for the inner-product reduction.
Let \(\bb,\be,\ee\) denote the respective multiplication costs.
Under schoolbook arithmetic,
\(\be=\kappa\bb\) and \(\ee=\kappa^2\bb\).
For these univariate bases and \(m\geq\kappa\), the reduction costs:
\[
    O(\kappa N)\,\bb+O(N)\,\be+O(N/\kappa)\,\ee
    =O(\kappa N)\,\bb.
\]
The prover accumulates row products directly into the auxiliary
coefficients. It stores the witness, a constant number of
length-\(m\) extension-field vectors, and \(O(\kappa)\) auxiliary
coefficients, using \(O(N+\kappa^2)=O(N)\) base-field coordinates.
The uncompressed extension-field proof uses
\(O(N\kappa^2)\) base-field operations and \(O(N\kappa)\) coordinates.
These bounds exclude the commitment scheme.
