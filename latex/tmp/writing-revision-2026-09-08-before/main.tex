\begin{abstract}
Provers in succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (SNARKs)
often incur large-field computation and memory costs even when
witness entries require only a few bits.
We present \methodname, a framework of packed arguments for
relations over small finite fields that exploits low-bit-width
witnesses to reduce both prover time and memory usage.
\methodname performs witness arithmetic over the small base field
while using extension-field challenges to achieve the desired soundness.

Our central technique is polynomial packing:
we transform a length-\(N=m\kappa\) vector relation over \(\Field\)
into a vector-valued polynomial identity and reduce it, by
evaluation at a random point, to a length-\(m\) vector relation
over a degree-\(\kappa\) extension \(\ExtensionField\) that meets the target soundness bound.
The prover performs the polynomial arithmetic over \(\Field\)
and proves the resulting shorter relation over \(\ExtensionField\).
We instantiate this reduction for inner and Hadamard products.
Assuming \(|\Field|\geq\kappa\) and precomputed packing bases,
both reductions require \(O(\kappa N)\) base-field operations
using schoolbook arithmetic and \(O(N)\) base-field elements
of prover memory.

We further develop a bitwidth-aware permutation argument and
polynomial commitment schemes (PCSs).
Together, these components yield a bitwidth-aware SNARK for
circuit satisfiability.
On circuits with \(2^{30}\) constraints and \(8\)-bit witnesses,
our protocol achieves an end-to-end speedup of \(XX\times\)
over the small-field-first approach of Plonky3, with both systems
using the same PCS.
\end{abstract}
