\section{Technical Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

Polynomial packing reduces the dimension of the relation processed
over the challenge field. For \(N=m\kappa\) and
\([\ExtensionField:\Field]=\kappa\), we encode a length-\(N\)
relation over \(\Field\) as a low-degree identity on length-\(m\)
vector polynomials. A random evaluation then yields a length-\(m\)
relation over \(\ExtensionField\).
The construction has two parts: an encoding that preserves the
relation and an opening protocol that binds its evaluated operands
to the original witness. We describe each in turn.

\subsection{Polynomial Packing of Vector Relations}
\label{sec:dualPhase}

Assume \(m,\kappa\) are powers of two and \(\kappa\geq2\).
Arrange the committed vectors \(\bm a,\bm b\in\Field^N\) as
\(\kappa\) columns of length \(m\):
\[
    \bm A=\concat{\bm a^{(1)},\ldots,\bm a^{(\kappa)}},
    \qquad
    \bm B=\concat{\bm b^{(1)},\ldots,\bm b^{(\kappa)}}
    \in\Field^{m\times\kappa}.
\]
Encode each row by a polynomial of degree below \(\kappa\).
For a basis
\(\bm\phi(X)=(\phi_1(X),\ldots,\phi_\kappa(X))^\top\), with
\(\phi_j\in\Field[X]\) of degree below \(\kappa\), define:
\begin{equation}
    \bm g_A(X)=\bm A\bm\phi(X),
    \qquad
    \bm g_B(X)=\bm B\bm\phi(X)
    \in\Field[X]^m.
\label{eq:overview-packing}
\end{equation}
Evaluation at \(\ext x\in\ExtensionField\) replaces each row of
\(\kappa\) base-field elements by one extension-field element:
\[
    \bm A\in\Field^{m\times\kappa}
    \quad\longmapsto\quad
    \bm g_A(X)
    \quad\xrightarrow{X=\ext x}\quad
    \ext{\bm a}=\bm g_A(\ext x)\in\ExtensionField^m.
\]
The basis determines how the original relation is recovered from
these polynomials. This leads to a two-step reduction.

\paragraph{Encode the relation as a polynomial identity.}
Choose the basis so that designated coefficients or interpolation
values of an expression in \(\bm g_A\) and \(\bm g_B\) encode
the original claim.
The prover supplies \(O(\kappa)\) auxiliary coefficients to specify
the remaining terms. Their prescribed form leaves the designated
coefficients or values unchanged.
We use monomial bases for inner products and Lagrange bases for
Hadamard products.

\paragraph{Evaluate to obtain the shorter relation.}
Once the auxiliary coefficients are fixed, the verifier samples an
extension-field challenge. Evaluating the identity gives a single
inner-product claim on length-\(m\) vectors, with a target computed
from the auxiliary polynomials.
An incorrect identity has a nonzero residual of degree \(O(\kappa)\)
after clearing negative powers, so it passes the random evaluation
with probability \(O(\kappa/|\ExtensionField|)\).
The inner-product proof uses \(O(m)\) extension-field operations
and leaves two multilinear-evaluation (MLE) claims on the packed
operands~\cite{asiacrypt/CCYY25}. Our opening protocol authenticates
these claims against the witness commitment.

\subsection{Instantiating the Packed Reduction}

\paragraph{Inner products.}
To encode \(c=\langle\bm a,\bm b\rangle\), assign opposite monomial
powers to matching columns. Choose
\(\bm\phi(X)=(1,X,\ldots,X^{\kappa-1})^\top\) and pair
\(\bm g_A(X)\) with \(\bm g_B(X^{-1})\).
For two blocks:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\langle\bm a^{(1)}+X\bm a^{(2)},
          \bm b^{(1)}+X^{-1}\bm b^{(2)}\rangle\\
 &\quad=
   \langle\bm a^{(1)},\bm b^{(1)}\rangle
   +\langle\bm a^{(2)},\bm b^{(2)}\rangle\\
 &\qquad\quad
   +X\langle\bm a^{(2)},\bm b^{(1)}\rangle
   +X^{-1}\langle\bm a^{(1)},\bm b^{(2)}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
The constant coefficient is the inner product, and every
cross-column term has a nonzero exponent.
For arbitrary \(\kappa\), the claim therefore holds if and only if
there exist
\(h_+,h_-\in\Field[X]\), each of degree at most \(\kappa-2\), satisfying:
\begin{equation*}
    \langle\bm g_A(X),\bm g_B(X^{-1})\rangle
    =c+Xh_+(X)+X^{-1}h_-(X^{-1}).
\end{equation*}
The degree bounds ensure that the auxiliary terms do not affect
the constant coefficient. After receiving \(h_+\) and \(h_-\),
the verifier samples
\(\ext x\in\ExtensionField^\times\).
Writing \(\ext{\bm a}=\bm g_A(\ext x)\) and
\(\ext{\bm b}=\bm g_B(\ext x^{-1})\), it remains to prove:
\[
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\rangle
    =c+\ext xh_+(\ext x)+\ext x^{-1}h_-(\ext x^{-1}).
\]
The right-hand side is known to the verifier, and the auxiliary
coefficients are sums of base-field cross-column products.

\paragraph{Hadamard products.}
For Hadamard products, the encoding must preserve each
matching-column product separately.
Given a committed \(\bm C\in\Field^{m\times\kappa}\), project
\(\bm C=\bm A\circ\bm B\) with random tensor weights
\(\ext{\bm l}\in\ExtensionField^m\) and
\(\ext{\bm r}\in\ExtensionField^\kappa\).
Each weight is a Kronecker product of two-entry MLE vectors.
The projection is thus a random evaluation of the residual's
multilinear extension, with error at most
\(\log_2N/|\ExtensionField|\).
We authenticate \(\ext c=\ext{\bm l}^{\top}\bm C\ext{\bm r}\) and prove:
\[
    \ext c=\ext{\bm l}^{\top}(\bm A\circ\bm B)\ext{\bm r}.
\]
The prover supplies claimed column products
\(\ext c_j=\langle\ext{\bm l},
\bm a^{(j)}\circ\bm b^{(j)}\rangle\), and the verifier checks
\(\ext c=\ext{\bm c}^{\top}\ext{\bm r}\).

Encode these column claims at distinct interpolation nodes.
Choose \(\kappa\) distinct nodes
\(\mathcal H=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\kappa\}\subseteq\Field\)
and the Lagrange basis satisfying
\(\psi_j(\alpha_i)=1\) for \(i=j\) and \(0\) otherwise.
Use \(\bm\phi=(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_\kappa)^\top\) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:overview-packing}, and set
\(g_C(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\ext c_j\psi_j(X)\) and
\(\Psi(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}(X-\alpha_j)\).
At \(\alpha_j\), the packed vectors recover column \(j\).
All column claims therefore hold if and only if the residual is
divisible by \(\Psi\):
\begin{equation*}
    \ext{\bm l}^{\top}
      \bigl(\bm g_A(X)\circ\bm g_B(X)\bigr)-g_C(X)
    =\Psi(X)h(X),
    \qquad \deg h\leq\kappa-2,
\end{equation*}
where \(h\in\ExtensionField[X]\).
Multiplication by \(\Psi\) fixes the residual at the interpolation
nodes, playing the role of the nonzero powers in the inner-product
encoding.
After \(\ext{\bm c}\) and \(h\) are fixed, the verifier samples
\(\ext x\in\ExtensionField\setminus\mathcal H\).
With \(\ext{\bm a}=\bm g_A(\ext x)\) and
\(\ext{\bm b}=\bm g_B(\ext x)\), the reduced claim is:
\[
    \langle\ext{\bm a},\ext{\bm b}\circ\ext{\bm l}\rangle
    =g_C(\ext x)+\Psi(\ext x)h(\ext x).
\]

Both constructions terminate in a length-\(m\) inner product over
\(\ExtensionField\), and hence use the same extension-field proof
and commitment interface.

\subsection{Prover Complexity}
\label{sec:highLevelEfficiency}

The saving depends on computing polynomial products before
evaluation. For inner products, the prover computes \(h_+\) and
\(h_-\) entirely over \(\Field\).
For Hadamard products, it first multiplies the row polynomials over
\(\Field\), then accumulates their coefficients with the
extension-field weights \(\ext{\bm l}\) and divides the residual
by \(\Psi\).
This order keeps the row multiplications over the base field even
though the weighted identity has extension-field coefficients.

With precomputed bases, these steps cost
\(O(m\kappa^2)=O(\kappa N)\) base-field operations and \(O(N)\)
mixed-field operations. Evaluating the packed vectors also costs
\(O(N)\) mixed-field operations, followed by \(O(m)\) extension-field
operations for the inner-product reduction.
Let \(\bb,\be,\ee\) denote the respective multiplication costs.
Under schoolbook arithmetic,
\(\be=\kappa\bb\) and \(\ee=\kappa^2\bb\).
For the bases above and \(m\geq\kappa\), the reduction costs:
\[
    O(\kappa N)\,\bb+O(N)\,\be+O(N/\kappa)\,\ee
    =O(\kappa N)\,\bb.
\]
The prover accumulates row products directly into the auxiliary
coefficients. It stores the witness, a constant number of
length-\(m\) extension-field vectors, and \(O(\kappa)\) auxiliary
coefficients, using \(O(N+\kappa^2)=O(N)\) base-field coordinates.
The uncompressed extension-field proof uses
\(O(N\kappa^2)\) base-field operations and \(O(N\kappa)\) coordinates.
These bounds exclude the commitment scheme.

\subsection{Authenticating Packed Evaluations}
\label{sec:highlevelPCS}

The relation reductions use operands \(\bm A\ext{\bm\rho}\) whose
packing weights depend on verifier challenges.
The witness commitment must be fixed before these challenges.
We therefore commit using a fixed basis and reduce
challenge-dependent evaluations to openings of that commitment.
Fix an \(\Field\)-basis
\(\ext{\bm\beta}=(\ext\beta_1,\ldots,\ext\beta_\kappa)^\top\)
of \(\ExtensionField\), and commit to
\(\ext{\bm a}=\bm A\ext{\bm\beta}\in\ExtensionField^m\).
This packing is injective over \(\Field^{m\times\kappa}\), so the
commitment binds every column of \(\bm A\).

\paragraph{From extension-field claims to base-field coordinates.}
A terminal claim has the form
\(\ext u=\langle\bm A\ext{\bm\rho},\ext{\bm\chi}\rangle\),
where \(\ext{\bm\rho}\in\ExtensionField^\kappa\) gives the public
packing weights and \(\ext{\bm\chi}\) is a public tensor query.
For the weighted Hadamard operand, absorb \(\ext{\bm l}\) into the query;
coordinatewise multiplication preserves its tensor structure.
The prover supplies \(\ext{\bm v}=\bm A^\top\ext{\bm\chi}\), and
the verifier checks \(\ext u=\ext{\bm\rho}^{\top}\ext{\bm v}\).

It remains to authenticate \(\ext{\bm v}\).
Its entries lie in \(\ExtensionField\), where the map that combines
them with \(\ext{\bm\beta}\) is no longer injective.
Instead, express the query and claimed values in base-field
coordinates:
\[
    \ext{\bm\chi}=\bm\Xi\ext{\bm\beta},
    \qquad
    \ext{\bm v}=\bm V\ext{\bm\beta},
    \qquad
    \bm\Xi\in\Field^{m\times\kappa},
    \quad \bm V\in\Field^{\kappa\times\kappa}.
\]
The column claims now form a base-field matrix identity.
Packing its rows is injective and exposes the committed vector:
\begin{equation*}
    \bm V=\bm A^\top\bm\Xi
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \ext{\bm\beta}^{\top}\bm V
    =\ext{\bm a}^{\top}\bm\Xi.
\end{equation*}
After \(\bm V\) is fixed, a uniform
\(\ext{\bm r}\in\ExtensionField^\kappa\) reduces this row identity
to one scalar claim, with error at most \(1/|\ExtensionField|\):
\[
    \ext{\bm\beta}^{\top}\bm V\ext{\bm r}
    =\langle\ext{\bm a},\bm\Xi\ext{\bm r}\rangle.
\]
An extension-field inner-product proof leaves an MLE opening of
\(\ext{\bm a}\) and a query-side claim
\(\ext w=\ext{\bm\xi}^{\top}\bm\Xi\ext{\bm r}\), where
\(\ext{\bm\xi}\in\ExtensionField^m\) is its terminal tensor weight.

\paragraph{Checking the public query.}
The columns of \(\bm\Xi\) need not retain the tensor structure of
\(\ext{\bm\chi}\). We therefore cannot check the query-side claim
by evaluating those columns succinctly.
Our solution is to reduce it to inner products whose terminal
weights lie in \(\Field\). For such weights, contraction commutes
with taking base-field coordinates, allowing us to recover the
required values from tensor evaluations.
Decompose \(\ext{\bm\xi}=\bm U\ext{\bm\beta}\), with
\(\bm U\in\Field^{m\times\kappa}\).
The prover sends a claimed product
\(\bm W=\bm U^\top\bm\Xi\in\Field^{\kappa\times\kappa}\);
the verifier checks \(\ext w=\ext{\bm\beta}^{\top}\bm W\ext{\bm r}\).
To check this base-field matrix product, the verifier independently
samples uniform
\(\bm s_{(i)},\bm t_{(i)}\in\Field^\kappa\) in each of \(k\)
repetitions after \(\bm W\) is fixed. The prover proves:
\[
    \bm s_{(i)}^\top\bm W\bm t_{(i)}
    =\langle\bm U\bm s_{(i)},\bm\Xi\bm t_{(i)}\rangle.
\]
Using base-field challenges in the inner-product reduction ensures
that its terminal tensor weight \(\bm l_{(i)}\) lies in \(\Field^m\).
The verifier recovers the two required rows as the coordinates of:
\[
    \langle\bm l_{(i)},\ext{\bm\xi}\rangle
       =(\bm l_{(i)}^\top\bm U)\ext{\bm\beta},
    \qquad
    \langle\bm l_{(i)},\ext{\bm\chi}\rangle
       =(\bm l_{(i)}^\top\bm\Xi)\ext{\bm\beta}.
\]
Each left-hand side is an inner product of tensor vectors and
costs \(O(\log m)\) extension-field operations to evaluate.
The verifier combines the recovered rows with \(\bm s_{(i)}\) and
\(\bm t_{(i)}\) to check the terminal values, without constructing
the full matrices \(\bm U\) and \(\bm\Xi\).

One repetition has error at most \((2\log_2m+2)/|\Field|\),
including the matrix projection and inner-product reduction.
For security parameter \(\lambda\), choose \(k\) so that
\(((2\log_2m+2)/|\Field|)^k\leq2^{-\lambda}\), assuming
\(|\Field|>2\log_2m+2\).
For \(m\geq\kappa\), the prover computes \(\bm W\) in
\(O(\kappa N)\) base-field operations and spends \(O(kN)\) on the
repetitions.
Sequential repetition uses \(O(N+\kappa^2)\) base-field coordinates
and requires no additional witness commitment.
